Why this replans every step

The car's state is expressed in Frenet (path) coordinates relative to the track centerline: progress s, lateral deviation e_y, heading error e_psi, and speed v. That turns "stay on track" into a simple bound on e_y and "go fast" into a speed-tracking cost, instead of needing a hand-designed reference trajectory — the same trick used in real racing-MPC research (Liniger, Domahidi & Morari, 2015) closely related to Berkeley's own BARC platform and MPC Lab.

At every control step, a 15-step, 2.25s-horizon optimal control problem is re-solved from the current state (receding-horizon control / MPC) subject to steering, acceleration, speed, and track-corridor limits, warm-started from the previous step's shifted solution. Only the first control is applied before the next re-solve — unlike the single open-loop solve in the powered-descent-guidance project, this controller is closing the loop in real time.

Move the sliders to change the target speed or drop an obstacle onto the track and watch the controller find a different line to hold the corridor.
